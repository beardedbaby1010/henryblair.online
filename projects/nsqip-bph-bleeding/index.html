Bleeding after TURP, photoselective vaporization and HoLEP

Henry M. Blair · Edward Wu · Michael Lee · Payam Hakimian

Laser procedures for benign prostatic hyperplasia are often chosen to limit bleeding, particularly in anticoagulated men. We compared 30-day bleeding after transurethral resection of the prostate (TURP), photoselective vaporization (PVP) and holmium laser enucleation (HoLEP), and described changes in procedure mix and transfusion over 13 years.

The question

Laser procedures are chosen to limit bleeding. Across thirteen years of national data, does that hold — and has bleeding fallen for every approach?

How it is being done

  • We identified TURP, PVP and HoLEP cases in the American College of Surgeons National Surgical Quality Improvement Program (ACS NSQIP) Participant Use File, 2012-2024.
  • Emergent cases, preoperative admission, moribund status, preoperative sepsis or ventilator dependence, and missing length of stay were excluded.
  • Outcomes were transfusion within 72 hours, reoperation and readmission for bleeding, a composite of the three, complications and readmission.
  • PVP and HoLEP were compared with TURP using unadjusted relative risks, and yearly transfusion was tested for linear trend.

What it shows so far

  • Of 123,020 procedures, 82,020 were TURP, 31,087 PVP and 9,913 HoLEP; HoLEP’s share rose from 5.8% to 14.8%.
  • Compared with TURP, PVP had lower transfusion (0.25% vs 0.90%; relative risk 0.28 (0.22–0.35)) and half the bleeding composite (1.4% vs 2.6%).
  • HoLEP had similar bleeding (2.5% vs 2.6%) but fewer serious complications (1.4% vs 1.7%) and readmissions (4.1% vs 5.3%).
  • Transfusion fell after all three: TURP 1.7% to 0.6%, PVP 0.5% to 0.0% (both p<0.001), HoLEP 2.5% to 0.5% (p=0.049).
PVP carried the lowest bleeding risk, while HoLEP matched TURP on bleeding with fewer complications and readmissions. Transfusion after surgery for benign prostatic hyperplasia has become rare nationally, declining for every procedure. It is likely that improved techniques, technology, and experience have contributed to this trend. Comparisons are unadjusted and do not account for prostate size.
