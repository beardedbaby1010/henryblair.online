Ketamine cystitis in the United States

Marc Ganz, MD · Michael West · Yitzchak Katlowitz · Henry M. Blair · Gina DeMeo · Christopher Edwards, MD · Arshia Sandozi, DO · Ariel Schulman, MD

Ketamine use has moved in a decade from club drug to prescribed treatment for depression and chronic pain. Its bladder toxicity is well described in case series and poorly described at national scale — which is a problem if prescribing keeps rising.

The question

How common is ketamine-associated cystitis in the United States, who develops it, and does its geography follow illicit supply or medical access?

How it is being done

  • We analysed the 2016–2021 National Inpatient Sample. Suspected ketamine cystitis was defined by concurrent ICD-10-CM diagnoses of a hallucinogen-related disorder and lower urinary tract symptoms.
  • NIS weights gave annual rates per 100,000 admissions and variation across Census divisions.
  • Drug Enforcement Administration Forensic Laboratory Information System ketamine reports were mapped by division and standardised to Census populations — a proxy for illicit supply.
  • Published retail-dispensing data and national ketamine-clinic counts were reviewed to put medical access beside it.

What it shows so far

  • An estimated 4,020 admissions were identified. Rates rose 140%, from 1.22 per 100,000 admissions in 2016 to 2.92 in 2021.
  • Mean age was 41.5 years; 66.5% were male, 52.2% had Medicaid, and 80.3% were treated at urban teaching hospitals.
  • Geographic admission patterns only partially overlapped those of 11,329 DEA ketamine reports from the same years.
  • Over the same period published retail data showed a 350% increase in patients dispensed ketamine from 2018–2023, and over 300 ketamine-focused clinics were listed nationally.
Admissions rose 140% with only partial geographic overlap with illicit ketamine reports. Against rapidly expanding retail dispensing and clinic access, illicit use alone may not explain the growing burden; medical exposure may be an under-recognised contributor and warrants direct investigation.
