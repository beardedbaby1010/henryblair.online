Half leave with opioids after urologic surgery

Henry M. Blair · Edward Wu · Ariel Schulman

Opioids prescribed at discharge are a modifiable source of persistent use and diversion. Using national data on discharge prescriptions, we described how often patients leave with an opioid after urologic surgery and how prescribing varies by procedure, surgical approach and patient characteristics.

The question

How often does a patient leave the hospital with an opioid after urologic surgery, and is that decided by the operation or by the patient?

How it is being done

  • We identified urologic procedures in the 2024 American College of Surgeons National Surgical Quality Improvement Program (ACS NSQIP) Participant Use File.
  • Emergent cases, preoperative admission, moribund status, preoperative sepsis or ventilator dependence, and missing length of stay were excluded.
  • The primary outcome was any opioid prescription at discharge; the secondary outcome was a prescription above 200 oral morphine milligram equivalents.
  • Rates were compared across procedures, approaches and patient groups with unadjusted two-proportion tests.
  • See the methods HTML file for more information — the written methods the pipeline generated for this cohort: every definition, exclusion and test it applied.

What it shows so far

  • Of 59,773 patients, 33,110 (55.4%) were discharged with an opioid.
  • Prescribing ranged from 26.0% after bladder tumor resection to 79.1% after kidney surgery and varied by approach (endoscopic 26.9%, open 65.7%, minimally invasive 75.1%).
  • Opioids were prescribed less to patients aged ≥ 70 (45.3% vs 62.2%), and more often to women (62.4% vs 53.8%) and inpatients (61.3% vs 51.5%; all p<0.001).
  • Readmission rates were similar in those with vs without an opioid (overall 5.8% vs 6.2%) but also varied by procedure (TURBT 5.7% vs 5.6%; Radical prostatectomy 4.1% vs 5.2%).
More than half of patients leave the hospital with an opioid after urologic surgery. Procedure type explains far more of the variation (26% to 79%) than any patient factor (45% to 62%). Low prescribing after endoscopic procedures suggests opioid-free discharge is feasible for many patients.
