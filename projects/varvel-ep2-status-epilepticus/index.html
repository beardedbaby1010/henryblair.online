Peripheral myeloid EP2 activation after status epilepticus

Nicholas H. Varvel, PhD (principal investigator) · Raymond Dingledine, PhD

Status epilepticus is followed by inflammation that does not simply accompany the injury but contributes to it. The question this line of work asks is which cells are responsible — brain-resident microglia, or myeloid cells recruited from the periphery — because only one of those is reachable by a drug that does not cross into the brain.

Status. Work in Dr. Nicholas Varvel's laboratory at Emory. Published as Varvel NH, Espinosa-Garcia C, Hunter-Chang S, et al., Journal of Neuroscience 2021;41(5):1105–1117.

The question

How much of the damaging inflammatory response after status epilepticus is driven by EP2 activation on peripheral myeloid cells rather than on brain-resident cells?

How it is being done

  • Mouse models of status epilepticus with genetic and pharmacologic manipulation of EP2 signalling.
  • Gene expression and PCR-based readouts of the inflammatory response.
  • Separation of peripheral myeloid contribution from brain-resident microglial contribution.
