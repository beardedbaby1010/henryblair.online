The national shift to same-day discharge after robotic prostatectomy

Edward Wu · Henry M. Blair · Ariel Schulman

Medicare removed laparoscopic radical prostatectomy from its Inpatient-Only List in January 2018, allowing same-day discharge. We described national uptake of same-day discharge after robot-assisted radical prostatectomy (RARP) and asked whether its growth has been accompanied by more readmissions or complications.

The question

Medicare opened the door to same-day discharge after prostatectomy in 2018. Did surgeons walk through it, and did readmissions rise when they did?

How it is being done

  • We identified RARP cases (laparoscopic or robotic; 94% robotic where recorded) in the American College of Surgeons National Surgical Quality Improvement Program (ACS NSQIP) Participant Use File, 2012-2024.
  • Emergent cases, preoperative admission, moribund status, preoperative sepsis or ventilator dependence, and missing length of stay were excluded.
  • Yearly same-day discharge rates were tested for linear trend.
  • Thirty-day outcomes were compared with overnight stay, and readmission between 2012-2018 and 2019-2024, using unadjusted relative risks.

What it shows so far

  • Of 119,220 patients, 7,088 (5.9%) went home the same day.
  • The rate stayed below 1% through 2018, then rose to 1.9% in 2019 and 15.6% in 2024 (p<0.001 for trend), including patients aged 70 or older (0.6% to 8.2%).
  • Compared with overnight stay, same-day discharge was associated with lower readmission (3.3% vs 4.2%; relative risk 0.78, 95% confidence interval 0.69-0.89), complications (5.0% vs 6.4%) and reoperation (0.5% vs 1.1%).
  • Overall readmission did not change between eras (4.1% vs 4.2%; relative risk 1.02, 0.97-1.08).
Same-day discharge after RARP grew sixteenfold after 2018 and now applies to about one in six patients, without a rise in national readmission rates. Lower complication rates after same-day discharge likely reflect selection of healthier patients, and comparisons are unadjusted.
