Too old or too frail? An analysis on robot-assisted simple prostatectomy

Edward Wu · Henry M. Blair · Ariel Schulman

Robot-assisted simple prostatectomy (RASP) is an established surgical approach for the treatment of large-gland benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH). However, perioperative outcomes following RASP among elderly and frail patients remain incompletely characterized. This study aimed to evaluate and compare 30-day postoperative complications following RASP across age and frailty subgroups.

The question

Age and frailty are routinely used to steer older men away from robotic simple prostatectomy. Do the outcomes justify that?

How it is being done

  • We identified robotic simple prostatectomies in 2022-2024 within the American College of Surgeons National Surgical Quality Improvement Program (ACS NSQIP).
  • Serious complications were defined as wound dehiscence/infection, organ space infections, pneumonia, re-intubation, pulmonary embolism, renal failure, cerebrovascular accident, cardiac arrest, sepsis, and septic shock.
  • Outcomes were compared with unadjusted relative risks and two-proportion or Fisher’s exact tests.
  • Subgroups were defined by frailty (Modified Frailty Index (mFI) ≥ 2) and elderly status (age ≥75 years).

What it shows so far

  • Of 2,580 men, 691 (26.8%) were aged 75 or older, 519 (20.5%) were frail and 182 (7.2% were both).
  • Older men had more complications overall (13.5% vs 10.3%; RR 1.3 95% confidence interval 1.03-1.64), transfusion (3.2% vs 1.3%), non-home discharge (2.0% vs 0.6%) and stays longer than one day (32.9% vs 25.8%), but similar serious complications (3.8% vs 3.4%) and readmission (5.6% vs 5.5%).
  • Frail men had longer stays (36.0% vs 25.6%).
  • Mortality was 0.1%.
In men aged 75 or older and frail men, RASP was associated with longer stays, more transfusion, and more non-home discharge. However, rates of serious complication, readmission, and mortality remained low and similar to younger, non-frail men. RASP has demonstrated safety in the frail and elderly population with comparable rates of serious complications, readmission, and mortality.
