Altered functional connectivity of the anterior insula in attention networks of autistic individuals

Henry M. Blair · Advisor: Opal Ousley, PhD · Committee: Gillian Hue, PhD · James Rilling, PhD

The anterior insula sits at the junction of the salience and ventral attention networks — the machinery that decides what is worth attending to. It has been implicated in autism for over a decade, with findings that contradict each other: hyperconnectivity in some cohorts, hypoconnectivity in others. One explanation for conflicting results is that a real effect is being averaged away.

Status. Bachelor of Science with Honors, Neuroscience and Behavioral Biology. Defended April 2023; archived in the Emory Theses and Dissertations repository.

The question

Does anterior insula connectivity with the salience and ventral attention networks differ in autism — and does it differ by sex, in a literature that has mostly treated sex as a covariate?

How it is being done

  • ROI-to-ROI resting-state functional connectivity between the anterior insula and pre-specified regions of the salience and ventral attention networks.
  • Males and females analyzed separately rather than pooled with sex as a covariate — the decision the whole thesis turns on.
  • Standard preprocessing across all scans, with ROIs defined a priori and MNI coordinates reported.
  • Connectivity matrices regressed against symptom instruments — SRS-2, SCQ and ADI-R — to test whether the differences carry behavioral meaning.

What it shows so far

  • Connectivity differed significantly between autistic and non-autistic children, concentrated at the temporoparietal junction and supramarginal gyrus.
  • The direction reversed by sex: increased connectivity in autistic females, decreased in autistic males — an effect that cancels toward null when the sexes are pooled.
  • Preliminary regressions related resting-state connectivity in autistic females to symptom scores, suggesting connectivity may carry information about severity.
Why the conflicting literature may be conflicting. If the effect runs in opposite directions by sex, then cohorts differing in sex composition will disagree — and averaging across sex will find less than either half contains.

What comes next

The sex-stratified result argues for replication in a larger cohort with enough autistic girls to power the female analysis on its own — the sample constraint that has shaped this literature from the start.
