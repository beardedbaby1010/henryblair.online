Robotic sacrocolpopexy versus transvaginal sacrospinous ligament fixation

Arsha Venkat · Edward Wu · Henry M. Blair · Allison Polland

Apical pelvic organ prolapse can be repaired abdominally with robotic sacrocolpopexy (SCP) or vaginally with sacrospinous ligament fixation (SSLF), with or without a hysterectomy. While vaginal native tissue repairs are often considered a lower risk option, comparative data on postoperative complications between these remain limited. This study compared 30-day postoperative complications.

The question

Vaginal native-tissue repair is often presented as the lower-risk option for apical prolapse. Does the national record agree?

How it is being done

  • We identified patients who underwent SCP (CPT 57425, designated robotic) or SSLF (CPT 57282) in 2022-2024 in the National Surgical Quality Improvement Program (NSQIP).
  • Serious complications were defined as wound dehiscence/infection, organ space infections, pneumonia, re-intubation, pulmonary embolism, renal failure, cerebrovascular accident, cardiac arrest, sepsis, and septic shock.
  • Other complications included superficial infections, UTI, deep venous thromboses, and blood transfusions.
  • Outcomes were compared with unadjusted relative risks (RR) and two-proportion or Fisher’s exact tests.

What it shows so far

  • Of 12,623 patients, 7,589 underwent robotic SCP and 5,034 SSLF; 89% and 94% had concomitant procedures.
  • Robotic SCP had fewer overall complications (6.6% vs 8.8%), reoperations (0.9% vs 1.5%) and stays >1 day (5.4% vs 14.0%).
  • Serious complications (1.4% vs 1.3%) and readmission (1.9% each) were similar.
  • UTI was less common after robotic SCP versus SSLF (3.6% vs 5.1%; RR 0.70, 95% CI 0.60-0.83).
  • On subgroup analysis of patients without concomitant procedures, UTI risk remained elevated for the SSLF group (RR 0.56 [0.41-0.77]).
Because SCP addresses all vaginal compartments, fewer SCP patients required concomitant procedures, potentially affecting outcomes. UTI differences suggest that the vaginal approach may influence UTI risk. However, unadjusted comparisons and differences in patient selection and prolapse severity may impact findings. Nonetheless, these results may help counsel patients hesitant about SCP.
