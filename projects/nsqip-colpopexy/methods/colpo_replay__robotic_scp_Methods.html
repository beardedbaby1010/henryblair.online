Data source: ACS NSQIP

Who collects the dataThe American College of Surgeons National Surgical Quality Improvement Program (ACS NSQIP) collects data at participating hospitals (roughly 700+ in recent years*). At each hospital, trained Surgical Clinical Reviewers (usually registered nurses) abstract the data from the medical record.
How the data are collectedCases are sampled systematically rather than chosen by the surgeon, so the file holds a sample of each hospital's operations, not every case. Reviewers record preoperative risk factors, operative details and outcomes using standard NSQIP definitions, and follow every patient for 30 days after surgery, including after discharge. Hospitals are audited for data reliability.**
WhenThe ACS releases one Participant Use File per calendar year, and definitions are revised from year to year. This study uses the annual Participant Use Files from 2022–2024.
NOTE: these data are de-identified; patient-level data stay on the analyst's computer. The pipeline only works if you have already obtained NSQIP access and have the data downloaded.

5

Exclusions

  • Emergent cases (n = 7)
    • Emergency operations carry extra risk of their own.
  • Already in hospital before the day of surgery (n = 52)
    • Patients already in hospital are sicker in ways the data can't fully capture, which could bias Robotic SCP vs SSLF.
  • ASA class 5 (moribund) (n = 0)
    • Moribund patients are expected to die with or without surgery, which would swamp any real difference between the groups.
  • Sepsis or septic shock before surgery (n = 35)
    • Sepsis before surgery causes complications on its own, so outcomes would reflect the infection more than Robotic SCP or SSLF.
  • On a ventilator before surgery (n = 0)
    • Patients already on a ventilator are critically ill; their outcomes reflect that illness more than the operation.
  • Length of stay missing (n = 0)
    • Without a length of stay, the length-of-stay, discharge and textbook outcomes can't be computed.

Excluding is not deleting

Excluded patients are left out of the main analyses only.

They remain in the workbook, flagged.

Every exclusion can be switched back on (re-included) in the spreadsheet model.

A patient can meet more than one criterion, so the counts can add up to more than 89.

How the groups are compared

Group 1 (reference)

SSLF

Group 2

Robotic SCP

Reading the results

  • Risk ratio (RR) = Group 2 ÷ Group 1. RR > 1 means the event was more frequent in Robotic SCP.
  • Risk difference (RD) = Group 2 − Group 1, in percentage points. RD > 0 means the event was more frequent in Robotic SCP.

Crude

  • Unadjusted.
  • Rates and comparisons computed from the raw counts in the database.

Overlap-weighted

  • Adjusted with a propensity score (overlap weighting, not matching): every patient stays in, with a weight.
  • The weights give the two groups the same mix of baseline characteristics (age, comorbidities, and so on).
  • Patients who could plausibly have been in either group count most.
  • Details on the statistics slides.
NOTE: adjustment can be switched on or off on every sheet of the workbook, so each figure and table states whether it shows adjusted numbers. Any future figure or table should state this too.

Geriatric variables (2021 onward)

Geriatric variables
  • Dementia, falls and living alone (baseline, Table 1)
  • Postoperative delirium and loss of independence at discharge (outcomes)
Why we look at them
  • Older patients make up a large share of many surgical cohorts.
  • The groups may differ in delirium and loss of independence, which standard complication outcomes miss.
  • Dementia, falls and living alone describe how frail each group was at baseline.
Absent before 2021
  • These fields are not in the Participant Use Files before 2021.
  • We did not remove them: those years are left blank, not counted as "no".
  • From 2021 they are filled in only for patients the hospital assessed (screened); for everyone else the field is blank and these patients are omitted from the rates which describe geriatric variables.

How the percentages are calculated

Each of these rates is calculated only among patients with a recorded answer. Patients with a blank field (before 2021, or not screened) are left out of that one variable's percentage, and stay in every other analysis.

Example: postoperative delirium % = patients with delirium ÷ patients screened for delirium.

10

Two primary outcomes

Why a correction is needed
  • Each outcome is tested on its own, but the conclusion (the groups differ) would be drawn if any primary outcome were significant.
  • Each test at α = 0.05 has a 5% chance of a false positive, so 2 tests give 2 chances:

1−(1−0.05)2≈9.75% chance of at least one false positive.

Bonferroni correction
  • Divide α by the number of primary outcomes (k), which keeps the overall false-positive chance at or below 5%:

αadjusted=αk=0.052=0.025

95% confidence intervals are shown unadjusted. Secondary outcomes are not corrected and are exploratory.

Significant if

p < 0.025

for each primary outcome

Acute kidney injury, graded (2022 onward)

  • KDIGO (Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes) is the international guideline group whose staging is the standard way to grade acute kidney injury (AKI).
  • It grades AKI by how much serum creatinine rises above the patient's baseline: stage 1 is mild, stage 3 is severe.

Stage 1 (not counted here)

Creatinine 1.5–1.9 × baseline

Stage 2

Creatinine 2.0–2.9 × baseline

Stage 3

Creatinine ≥ 3 × baseline, or a rise to ≥ 4.0 mg/dL

  • We graded AKI from NSQIP's creatinine-rise field* among patients with renal insufficiency or failure.
  • The field exists only from 2022, so earlier years are blank, not zero.

* POSTOP_CREAT_MSINCR: the NSQIP variable (2022 onward) that records how much postoperative creatinine rose above baseline. The pipeline maps it to KDIGO stages 2 and 3.

Adjustment: propensity score with overlap weights

1. Propensity score

  • A logistic regression estimates each patient's probability of being in Robotic SCP (Group 2), from the characteristics below.
  • Missing values are kept as their own category.

2. Overlap weights

  • Group 2 patients get weight 1 − PS; Group 1 patients get PS.
  • This balances every characteristic in the model exactly.
  • No patient is dropped (unlike matching).

3. Weighted comparison

  • Weighted rates
  • Risk ratios with robust (sandwich) 95% CIs
  • Wald p-values

Sensitivity analyses

Inverse-probability weights

  • The same propensity model with IPTW weights instead of overlap weights.
  • If the answer agrees, it does not depend on the weighting method.

Alternative cohorts

  • The main comparison re-run in restricted cohorts (elective only, age ≥ 65, ASA 1–2 and others).
  • If the answer agrees, it is not driven by one kind of patient.

Multivariable modified Poisson

  • Poisson regression (log link) of each key outcome on the group and every propensity-model characteristic.
  • Robust (HC0) 95% CIs give adjusted risk ratios without weighting.

E-values and numbers needed to treat

E-value

The required strength of an unmeasured confounder to fully explain away the observed association between the exposure and the outcome.

That is, how strongly an omitted factor must relate to both the group assignment and the outcome to invalidate the study's conclusions.

E=RR+RR×(RR−1)

For RR < 1, use 1 ÷ RR.

NNT / NNH

The expected number of patients to treat with Group 2 instead of Group 1 to prevent (or cause) a single additional event.

That is, the reciprocal of the absolute risk difference: how many patients must switch groups to see one fewer or one more event.

NNT=1|RD|

Subgroup analyses

Levels

Crude risk ratios for Urinary tract infection and Serious complication within each level of:

  • Age – cut at 65 and 75
  • BMI – cut at 30 and 40
  • Frailty (mFI-5 score) – cut at 1 and 2
  • ASA class – cut at 3 and 4

Heterogeneity

  • Cochran's Q test (p for interaction) asks whether the Robotic SCP vs SSLF effect differs across levels.
  • Cut-points can be changed on the workbook's Settings tab.
21

Observed vs expected outcomes

What is compared

  • Observed events in each group
  • vs NSQIP's own risk-model predictions (each patient's expected probability of death and of morbidity, summed)
  • Reported as O/E ratios

Reading O/E

  • O/E below 1: fewer events than the patients' risk predicted.
  • O/E above 1: more.
  • The 95% CI uses an exact Poisson interval on the observed count.
22

Missing data, software and reproducibility

Missing data

  • Variables are kept as recorded; no values are imputed.
  • Fields that did not exist in a year are blank, not zero.
  • Patients missing a variable are left out of that variable's denominator only.

Software

  • Cohort extraction and the propensity model: Python (pandas, statsmodels).
  • Every table and test is a live formula in the workbook; changing a setting updates every number.
  • All choices are saved in the project's answers file, so the study can be rebuilt exactly.
SUMMARY
  • Using ACS NSQIP 2022–2024, this study compares Robotic SCP (Group 2) with SSLF (Group 1, the reference).
  • 6 exclusions (emergent cases, already in hospital before the day of surgery, asa class 5 (moribund), sepsis or septic shock before surgery, on a ventilator before surgery, length of stay missing) leave 12,623 of 20,125 patients for the main analyses.
  • The primary outcomes, Urinary tract infection and Serious complication, are judged at p < 0.025 (Bonferroni).
  • All 34 outcomes are analyzed crude and overlap-weighted.
