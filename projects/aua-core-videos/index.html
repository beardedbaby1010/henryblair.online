Sacral neuromodulation placement

Runtime 7:58 · submitted August 31st, 2026

Percutaneous placement of a peripheral nerve evaluation (PNE) lead for sacral neuromodulation, using the Medtronic InterStim system in a patient with refractory overactive bladder.

What the video walks through

  • Patient positioning
  • Fluoroscopic localization of the S3 foramen
  • Needle and lead placement guided by motor and sensory response
  • Securing the lead and connecting an external trial stimulator
Sacral neuromodulation is a reversible, office-based option for patients who have failed conservative and pharmacologic therapy, and a successful trial predicts response to permanent implantation. The video is built around the anatomic landmarks and the troubleshooting points a trainee actually needs.

Vaginal pessaries: types and fitting

Runtime 7:09 · submitted August 31st, 2026

Pessaries are a safe, effective, first-line, non-surgical option for pelvic organ prolapse and stress incontinence — and many trainees never see a fitting performed. That gap is the reason this video exists.

What the video walks through

  • The three pessary families — support, space-filling, and incontinence — and the six most commonly used types
  • A live fitting encounter: positioning, antiseptic prep, and trial of two device sizes
  • Anatomic predictors of a harder fit: enlarged genital hiatus, posterior-predominant prolapse, short vaginal length, advanced-stage prolapse
  • Follow-up, self-management, and complication management for safe long-term use

The question

How do you teach a procedure whose difficulty is entirely in the hands — where to stand, what to feel for, what the wrong answer looks like — to someone who cannot be in the room?

How it is being done

  • Each video is scripted section by section against the AUA's teaching-video format, with every line of narration mapped to the specific footage, still or diagram it plays over.
  • Anatomic and procedural diagrams were built from scratch rather than sourced, because the AUA submission requires clear rights to every element on screen.
  • Filmed at 1080p and cut to sit under the AUA's eight-minute limit — long enough to show the judgment, short enough that a trainee will actually watch it.
  • Each submission is a package: the video, a written abstract, and a signed non-exclusive license.
