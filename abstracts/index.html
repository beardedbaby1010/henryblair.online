Health Services Research: Quality Improvement & Patient Safety

Bone-density screening is triggered by prior bone disease rather than androgen-deprivation therapy itself

Henry M. Blair · Owen Hallauer · Alexis Narvaez-Rojas · Ariel Schulman · James Butler

Of 100 older men starting ADT, 13 were screened within a year, and 3 started an antiresorptive. Screening clustered in men with pre-ADT bone disease or an early fracture, and screened men’s excess fracture risk vanished after matching, suggesting disease prompts screening, not the reverse. Men without known disease are largely missed; starting ADT does not prompt screening, but it should.

Prostate Cancer: Advanced (including drug therapy)

Trends in treatment intensification for metastatic castration-sensitive prostate cancer in men aged 70 or older, 2017-2023

Henry M. Blair · Owen Hallauer · Alexis Narvaez-Rojas · Ariel Schulman · James Butler

ARPIs both replaced docetaxel and expanded the treated population, yet in 2023, nearly half (47.6%) of older men with mCSPC still started ADT alone, and men with visceral metastases were no more likely to be intensified despite the stronger indication. This gap has improved markedly in recent years but remains a clear target for quality improvement in the prostate cancer care of older men.

Sexual Function/Dysfunction: Medical, Hormonal & Non-surgical Therapy

Overburdened and undertreated: Breast cancer patients carry the highest genitourinary symptom burden but the lowest vaginal estrogen use.

Henry M. Blair · Allison Polland · Arshia Sandozi, DO

Women with a breast cancer history carry the greatest symptom burden yet are least likely to be treated, initiating a quarter less often than women without Z85.3 codes. Those treated are the more symptomatic, and ER+ and ER- women initiate similarly though the concern applies only to ER+. Undertreatment reflects blanket caution, not individualized risk, and persisted after the FDA announcement.

Improving Access and Outcomes Across All Populations

Rise in vaginal estrogen use after the FDA announcement widened the absolute racial treatment gap

Henry M. Blair · Allison Polland · Arshia Sandozi, DO

Black and Native Hawaiian or Pacific Islander women initiate vaginal estrogen at roughly half the rate of white women, and a favorable regulatory change left that ratio nearly unchanged; in absolute terms, the gap widened by 42%. Only the Hispanic-non-Hispanic difference closed materially. These data describe women already in care and cannot separate under-diagnosis from under-treatment.

Health Policy Research

Vaginal estrogen prescribing accelerated after the Food and Drug Administration boxed-warning announcement, independent of formulation labeling

Henry M. Blair · Allison Polland · Arshia Sandozi, DO

Vaginal estrogen prescribing was climbing beforehand, the announcement accelerated its rate rather than causing a sudden jump. The rise survived calibration to unrelated controls but not reanchoring to non-announcement months. The relabeled formulation lost share of incident use. Thus, prescribing appears to have responded to the FDA’s announcement, rather than the relabeling itself.

Half Leave With Opioids: Is Urologic Surgery Overprescribing Pain Medication? An ACS NSQIP Analysis of 2024 Data

Henry M. Blair · Edward Wu · Ariel Schulman

More than half of patients leave the hospital with an opioid after urologic surgery. Procedure type explains far more of the variation (26% to 79%) than any patient factor (45% to 62%). Low prescribing after endoscopic procedures suggests opioid-free discharge is feasible for many patients.

Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia: Surgical Therapy & New Technology

National Trends in Bleeding and Perioperative Outcomes After TURP, Photoselective Vaporization, and HoLEP: An ACS NSQIP Analysis, 2012–2024

Henry M. Blair · Edward Wu · Michael Lee · Payam Hakimian

PVP carried the lowest bleeding risk, while HoLEP matched TURP on bleeding with fewer complications and readmissions. Transfusion after surgery for benign prostatic hyperplasia has become rare nationally, declining for every procedure. It is likely that improved techniques, technology, and experience have contributed to this trend. Comparisons are unadjusted and do not account for prostate size.

The Rapid Rise of Robot-Assisted Simple Prostatectomy, Less Blood, Fewer Complications: National Utilization Trends and Perioperative Outcomes, 2022-2024

Health Services Research: Quality Improvement & Patient Safety

Too Old or Too Frail for Robot-Assisted Enucleation Prostatectomy? An ACS NSQIP Analysis, 2022–2024

Edward Wu · Henry M. Blair · Ariel Schulman

In men aged 75 or older and frail men, RASP was associated with longer stays, more transfusion, and more non-home discharge. However, rates of serious complication, readmission, and mortality remained low and similar to younger, non-frail men. RASP has demonstrated safety in the frail and elderly population with comparable rates of serious complications, readmission, and mortality.

What Do Patients with Nocturia Want Treated? Patient-Defined Goals and Tolerance Beyond Void Count

Oliver B. Menken · Benjamin H. Parker · Henry M. Blair · Isabel X. Guan · Jeffrey P. Weiss

Nocturia treatment success cannot be defined by void count alone. Vaginal Estrogen the project →

Vaginal estrogen prescribing before and after the FDA announcement on removing boxed warnings

Henry M. Blair · Allison Polland · Arshia Sandozi, DO

Prescribing was already climbing 22.0% a year before the FDA acted. There was no step change at November 2025; the trend steepened to 71.1% a year. Negative-control drugs accelerated over the same months, and after adjusting for that background the increase fell to 50.7% a year (p=0.070). Roughly half the apparent effect is the pre-existing trend — which is the methodological point: without a prior-year comparison and unrelated controls, this effect would have been overstated more than two-fold.

Breast cancer: overburdened and undertreated — highest genitourinary symptom burden and lowest vaginal estrogen use

Henry M. Blair · Allison Polland · Arshia Sandozi, DO

Across 7.9 million women in care, breast cancer history and aromatase inhibitor use were the only characteristics for which vaginal estrogen initiators were depleted rather than enriched. Yet initiators with breast cancer carried more atrophic vaginitis, UTI and incontinence than initiators without it. Initiation ran 54.3 per 10,000 for breast cancer patients and 43.7 for AI users against 62.2 overall. Their symptoms are worse, not better, so the gap is not explained by less need.

Vaginal estrogen underuse after breast cancer is independent of receptor status

Henry M. Blair · Allison Polland · Arshia Sandozi, DO

If prescribers were acting on the estrogen-sensitivity concern, receptor-positive women would be treated less often than receptor-negative women. They are not: RR 0.91 before the FDA announcement (p=0.30) and 0.99 after (p=0.90), with both groups rising comparably. Women with receptor-negative disease, for whom the theoretical concern does not apply, are undertreated nearly as much. The shortfall follows the diagnosis, not the tumor biology.

Announcement, not label: vaginal estrogen prescribing after recent FDA actions is not formulation-specific

Henry M. Blair · Allison Polland · Arshia Sandozi, DO

A natural experiment: in February 2026 the estradiol vaginal ring was the only vaginal estrogen to be relabeled. If prescribers follow the label, ring use should gain. It lost — 14.0% to 12.3% of classified starts (RR 0.88) — while cream, whose label never changed, gained the difference. Clinicians acted on the news, not the document. A decade of class-wide caution is unlikely to be undone one label at a time.
