Urinary retention after Retzius-sparing prostatectomy

Retzius-sparing robotic prostatectomy gives men better early continence, but preserving the anterior compartment may cost postoperative retention — and no study has examined retention as a primary outcome. In ~200 patients I am measuring its incidence and testing predictors: membranous urethral length, intravesical prostatic protrusion, prostate volume, baseline LUTS, PSA, diabetes, frailty, and peri-operative alpha-blocker, 5-ARI and ADT use.

Can women self-estimate their post-void residual?

AUA guidelines require a post-void residual measurement to diagnose overactive bladder — which needs an in-office ultrasound, at odds with the telemedicine pathway the same guidelines endorse. Women could not estimate their own PVR reliably; but incomplete emptying was rare and no woman met criteria for retention, so the in-office requirement may be safely deferred for low-risk women, opening the door to telemedicine.

Does neighborhood deprivation predict urogynecologic access in Brooklyn?

A companion overactive-bladder study testing whether the Area Deprivation Index and travel burden relate to access and disease severity — and whether ADI is even valid in a dense, transit-rich city. It did not track access or severity in Brooklyn, likely because public transit blunts the travel barriers seen elsewhere; disadvantaged women were, however, more likely to report urge frequency.

Altered functional connectivity of the anterior insula in attention networks

The anterior insula has been studied in autism for years with conflicting results. Analyzing sex separately, I measured ROI-to-ROI resting-state connectivity between the anterior insula and the salience and ventral attention networks. Connectivity differed by sex in opposite directions — increased in autistic girls, decreased in autistic boys — most clearly at the temporoparietal junction and supramarginal gyrus, and preliminary regressions related that connectivity to symptom scores (SRS-2, SCQ, ADI-R). A finding that argues sex belongs in the analysis rather than in the covariates. Defended April 2023.

Traumatic stress at the moment of brain injury and the risk of post-traumatic epilepsy

Traumatic brain injury can set off epileptogenesis — the months or years of neuronal loss, mossy-fiber sprouting and shifting excitation that precede a first unprovoked seizure. Clinically, severe stress at the moment of injury is associated with worse outcomes, but preclinical work had studied stress and injury separately and never together. Adult male rats were submerged for 45 seconds before a controlled cortical impact; controls swam freely before the same impact. Open-field and elevated-plus-maze testing confirmed the stress manipulation, then flurothyl seizure thresholds were measured at five and ten days. At five days the groups were indistinguishable; by ten days the stressed animals had a lower threshold for tonic-clonic seizures (p = 0.02) and seized longer — the interaction emerging inside the epileptogenic window rather than at the moment of injury.

Estrogen and the dentate gyrus: response to spatial and environmental novelty

Roughly 200,000 oophorectomies are performed in the United States each year, each one producing surgical menopause and a sudden drop in estradiol; many of those women then take estrogen therapy partly for cognition. Estrogen receptors are dense in the dentate gyrus, the structure behind pattern separation, and in vitro work in the lab had shown estradiol altering long-term potentiation there — but the behavioral consequence was open. Eighteen ovariectomized rats received β-estradiol (2 µg/day for four days) or oil, then met an environmental change (a new arena) and a metric change (objects moved from 30 to 50 cm). Estradiol-replaced animals explored significantly less after the environmental change (p = 0.028), consistent with estradiol shaping dentate-mediated learning. Written up for the Junior Science and Humanities Symposium and presented as a poster — my first study, run as a high-school student in the STAR program, and the first time I worked on what estrogen does after it is taken away.
